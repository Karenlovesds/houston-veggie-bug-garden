# Squash Bugs (Anasa tristis)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** late spring → fall
- **Severity:** medium–high; can kill young squash

## Identification

![Adult squash bug](../images/bugs/squash-bugs.jpg)
*Adult squash bug. [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anasa_tristis,_adult.jpg) — Ilona Loser, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

- Adults: flat, gray-brown, ~1.5 cm, with a narrow neck.
- Nymphs: gray-green with spiky legs, often in groups on stems.
- Eggs: shiny copper ovals in rows on leaf undersides.

### Look-alikes
- Stink bugs: broader shield shape.
- Leaf-footed bugs: have flattened leaf-like legs.

## Host plants

Squash, pumpkins, zucchini, cucumbers, melons.

## Signs of damage

1. Yellowing, wilting leaves that turn brown and crisp.
2. Nymph clusters on leaf undersides.
3. Pumpkin vines collapse in late summer.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Adults overwinter in debris and return in May. Eggs hatch in about 10 days. Multiple generations through the long Houston season; adults keep piling up.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Check leaf undersides weekly from July — look for egg masses and nymphs (Refs: Clemson, UMN).

### Prevent
- Plant tolerant varieties (butternut/buttercup squash).
- Clean up old vines in fall — adults overwinter there.
- Control weeds (Refs: Clemson, UMN).

### Physical
- Flip and crush egg masses (best 1-minute control).
- Trap boards: place a board on the ground; bugs hide under it; flip and kill them.
- Knock adults and nymphs into soapy water (Refs: Clemson, UMN).

### Biological
- Conserve predators and parasites — avoid broad-spectrum sprays (Refs: Clemson, UMN).
### Least-toxic products

![Spinosad](../images/products/placeholder/spinosad.svg) *Spinosad — add your own product photo.*

- Spinosad covers young nymphs — spray undersides, late in the day to protect bees (Refs: Clemson, UMN).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Insecticides struggle to kill adults — time any spray on young nymphs per label (Ref: Clemson).

## Field notes (personal)

- Copper-egg rub-off is the single best 2-minute fix in a Houston squash bed.
- Board traps caught ~60 adults a night at peak.
