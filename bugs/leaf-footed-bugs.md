# Leaf-Footed Bugs (Leptoglossus spp.)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** June–November, worst on late tomatoes/peppers
- **Severity:** medium — spoils fruit

## Identification

![Adult leaf-footed bug](../images/bugs/leaf-footed-bugs.jpg)
*Adult leaf-footed bug. [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leptoglossus_phyllopus.jpg) — Russ Ottens, CC BY 3.0 US.*

- Elongate brown/cinnamon bug ~2 cm.
- Key ID: flattened, leaf-shaped hind legs.
- Nymphs: red-orange bodies on fruit.

### Look-alikes
- Stink bugs: shield-shaped, no leaf feet.
- Assassin bugs: predators — no leaf feet; do NOT kill them.

## Host plants

Tomatoes, peppers, squash, citrus, peaches.

## Signs of damage

1. Sunken, corky spots on fruit where they fed.
2. Fruit opens secondary rot at the punctures.
3. Blemished late tomatoes and peppers.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Eggs laid in chains on stems; nymphs mature in 5–8 weeks (Ref: UF). Adults overwinter and adults are active all fall. Start watching when tomatoes bloom (Ref: TX A&M).

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Check leaf undersides, stems, and fruit daily once tomatoes bloom (Refs: TX A&M, UF).

### Prevent
- Keep tall grass and weeds away from the garden — they hide there (Refs: TX A&M, UF).

### Physical
- Hold a tray of soapy water under a cluster and shake the plant — bugs fall in.
- Hand-pick egg chains off stems.
- Hand-vacuum the nymphs (Refs: TX A&M, UF).

### Biological
- Assassin bugs, spiders, birds, and parasitic wasps eat them. Protect these helpers.

### Least-toxic products
- Target sprays at the nymph stage only. Spot-treat; adults are hard to control (Ref: UF).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Avoid broad-spectrum sprays — they kill bees and beneficials (Ref: UF).

## Field notes (personal)

- A 15-minute dusk sweep with a butterfly net beat any spray all season.
