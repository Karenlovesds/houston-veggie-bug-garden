# Cucumber Beetles (Acalymma vittatum, Diabrotica ssp.)

- **Type:** chewing
- **Houston timing:** late spring → fall
- **Severity:** high — they spread bacterial wilt

## Identification

- Striped: yellow-orange, three black stripes down the back, ~6 mm.
- Spotted: green-yellow with 12 black dots.

### Look-alikes
- Lady beetles: round, domed, no stripes like this.
- Flea beetles: tiny, jump, make small round holes.

## Host plants

Cucumbers, squash, pumpkins, melons, beans.

## Signs of damage

1. Round chew marks on leaf edges.
2. Beetles scatter when you brush the plant.
3. Sudden vine wilt = bacterial wilt, spread by these beetles. Plants usually die (Ref: Clemson, UC IPM).

## Lifecycle in Houston

Adults overwinter near last year's beds. Larvae feed on roots for a few weeks, then new adults emerge. Many overlapping generations in the warm season.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Check new growth weekly. Beetles attack seedlings the moment they emerge (Ref: Clemson).

### Prevent
- Plant bacterial-wilt-resistant cucurbit varieties.
- Remove weeds around the bed (Refs: Clemson, UC IPM).

### Physical
- Row cover over young plants is the main defense. Remove it at flowering so bees can pollinate (Refs: Clemson, UC IPM).

### Biological
- Predators and parasites help; mature plants tolerate a lot of feeding (Ref: UC IPM).

### Least-toxic products
- Spinosad is labeled for cucumber beetles on cucurbits (Ref: Clemson).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Carbaryl/bifenthrin/permethrin per label only if beetles are severe (Ref: Clemson).

## Field notes (personal)

- One year without row cover: bacterial wilt killed 6 of 7 cucumbers in 5 days.
- Fall planting (August) dodges the worst beetles.
