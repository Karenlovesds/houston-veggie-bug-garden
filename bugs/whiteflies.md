# Whiteflies (Aleyrodidae)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** year-round; peaks late summer–fall
- **Severity:** medium–high

## Identification

- Adults: tiny (<2 mm), pale, fly off in a white cloud when you shake the plant.
- Immatures: flat scale-like disks stuck to leaf undersides.

### Look-alikes
- Aphids: green, on new growth, no white cloud.
- Scale: immobile bumps, never fly.

## Host plants

Tomatoes, peppers, brassicas, squash, hibiscus, citrus.

## Signs of damage

1. White cloud when plants are disturbed.
2. Pale stippling on leaves.
3. Sticky honeydew + sooty mold.
4. Wilting in heat despite watering.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Egg to adult in about 3 weeks in summer. All stages present year-round in mild winters. Worst August–October.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Shake plants weekly; count the cloud.
- Check leaf undersides. Don't wait for visible damage (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Prevent
- Inspect new transplants before buying — many arrive infested.
- Keep weeds down and ants out (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Physical
- Vacuum adults early in the morning when they're slow.
- Insecticidal soap against immature stages; repeat weekly.
- Silver reflective mulch repels adults (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Biological
- Parasitic wasps and lacewings help but won't stop a big outbreak.
- Start early, at low populations (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Least-toxic products
- Insecticidal soap or horticultural/neem oil. Cover all undersides.
- Repeat 3× spaced a week apart. Avoid overuse — resistance builds fast (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Avoid broad-spectrum sprays — whiteflies are resistant to many and you'll lose the beneficials (Ref: UC IPM).

## Field notes (personal)

- A tomato bed next to an infested hibiscus was a whitefly highway — keep those apart.
- The "shake test" at the nursery has saved me 3 tomato seasons.
