# Fire Ants (Solenopsis invicta)

- **Type:** biting/stinging
- **Houston timing:** year-round; worst April–June and after rain
- **Severity:** high for comfort and young plants

## Identification

- Reddish-brown ants 2–6 mm; workers come in different sizes.
- Fresh dome-shaped dirt mounds after rain.
- Sting = painful, burning sensation and a small blister-like pustule.

### Look-alikes
- Crazy ants: small, fast, erratic, no mound, no sting.

## Host plants

Your feet, ankles, kids, pets — and the crowns of young transplants.

## Signs of damage

1. Fresh dirt mounds that rebuild overnight.
2. Burning stings with pustules.
3. Swarms on spilled food/seed.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Colonies are perennial. Queens lay a thousand eggs a day in summer. Mild winters mean no real die-off — spring rains trigger new mounds, and nuptial flights release swarms each summer.

## Control (IPM)

### Goal
Suppression, not eradication. Fire ants re-invade; no permanent fix exists (Ref: TX A&M).

### Monitor
- Treat where heavy: about 5+ mounds per quarter acre.
- With just a few mounds, treat them individually to spare native ants (Ref: TX A&M).

### The official Two-Step Method (TX A&M)
1. Broadcast a fire-ant bait over the whole yard 1–2×/year, best late August–mid-October.
2. A few weeks later, treat remaining nuisance mounds individually (drench, granule, bait, or dust) (Ref: TX A&M).

### Least-toxic products
- Broadcast baits are the low-tox route. Fast-acting baits: spinosad, hydramethylnon, indoxacarb.
- Slow-acting (longer-lasting) baits: abamectin, methoprene, pyriproxyfen (Ref: TX A&M).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Individual mound treatments: liquid drenches, dusts, granules. Follow the label.

## Field notes (personal)

- Spinosad bait in May and September dropped colony counts ~80% in one season.
