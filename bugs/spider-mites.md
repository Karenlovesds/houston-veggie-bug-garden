# Spider Mites (Tetranychidae)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** hot, dry stretches (June–August)
- **Severity:** medium–high

## Identification

- Mites are ~0.4 mm — you mainly see the damage and webbing.
- Tap a leaf over white paper: red/rust specks that crawl = mites.
- Fine webs across stems and leaf veins.

### Look-alikes
- Thrips: dark slivers that hop, no webbing.
- Heat/drought stress: looks similar but no stippling or webs.

## Host plants

Tomatoes, peppers, squash, beans, roses, hibiscus.

## Signs of damage

1. Pale/bronze stippling on the leaf tops.
2. Fine webbing at stem-and-leaf joints.
3. Leaves curl, crisp, and drop.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Egg to adult in 5–7 days at 90°F. Mites are arachnids, not insects — most *insect*icides don't work on them and can make them worse (Ref: UC IPM).

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Tap leaves over white paper weekly in July–August.
- Check leaf undersides before treating (Ref: UC IPM).

### Prevent
- Water stress is fuel. Keep plants evenly watered and mulched.
- Keep dust down — dust favors mites (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Physical
- Strong water spray to the leaf undersides. Repeat 2–3× per week.
- Quarantine new plants.

### Biological
- Predatory mites, lacewings, lady beetles, minute pirate bugs.
- Avoid broad-spectrum sprays, which trigger mite flare-ups (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Least-toxic products
- Insecticidal soap and horticultural/neem oil — must contact mites, cover undersides, repeat.
- Don't spray water-stressed plants or above 90°F (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Selective miticides only, on ornamentals. Rotate products to slow resistance (Ref: TX A&M).

## Field notes (personal)

- The hot dry zone next to the south wall is always the first infestation site.
