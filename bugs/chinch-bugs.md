# Chinch Bugs (Blissus insularis)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** June–September heat
- **Severity:** high for St. Augustine lawns

## Identification

- Adults: ~3–4 mm, black with white crossed wings.
- Nymphs: orange/red with a light band.
- They run fast through the thatch.

### Look-alikes
- Ants: pinched waist; chinch bugs are straight-bodied.
- Drought stress: similar yellow patches, but no bugs in the thatch.

## Host plants

St. Augustine (major), zoysia, bermuda.

## Signs of damage

1. Irregular yellow-to-straw patches that spread.
2. Patches keep growing even when watered.
3. Bugs found at the green/brown border in the thatch (Ref: TX A&M).

## Lifecycle in Houston

Adults overwinter in thatch. Nymphs hatch in May; Houston can stack 3–5 generations a season. Hot and dry weather spurs the worst damage.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- At the edge of the damaged patch, part the thatch and look for bugs (Ref: TX A&M).
- Re-check treated spots every 3–5 days for 2 weeks (Ref: TX A&M).

### Prevent
- Keep thatch thin — thatch shelters them.
- Avoid over-fertilizing.
- Plant resistant cultivars; Floratam has high resistance (Ref: TX A&M).

### Physical
- Dethatch the lawn (physically removes their shelter) (Ref: TX A&M).

### Biological
- No reliably effective fungal control yet. Preserve beneficial insects (Ref: TX A&M).

### Least-toxic products
- Spot-treat only the damaged area and its margin — not the whole lawn (Ref: TX A&M).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Labeled liquid/granular insecticides, watered into the canopy, timed before eggs hatch and when damage appears (Ref: TX A&M).

## Field notes (personal)

- The can-flotation test settled 3 lawn debates: people blamed watering; it was chinch.
