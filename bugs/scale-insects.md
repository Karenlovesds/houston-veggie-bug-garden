# Scale Insects (Coccoidea)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** year-round; worst on citrus, hollies, crape myrtles
- **Severity:** medium–high on citrus

## Identification

![Black scale bumps on a stem](../images/bugs/scale-insects.jpg)
*Scale bumps (black scale) on a stem. [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saissetia_oleae_338167960.jpg) — Dustin, CC BY 4.0.*

- They look like motionless bumps on stems and leaves.
- Armored scale: hard shell; flicks off leaving the leaf clean underneath.
- Soft scale: rounded wax bumps; sticky when scraped (alive underneath).

### Look-alikes
- Mealybugs: white cottony, near stem joints.
- Bark/cork nodes: don't flick off as a shell (they're plant tissue).

## Host plants

Citrus, hollies, crape myrtle, gardenias, camellias, roses.

## Signs of damage

1. Sticky honeydew and black sooty mold under the plant.
2. Bumps on stems and leaf undersides.
3. Yellowing leaves, thinning branches.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Eggs hatch into tiny crawlers (the only mobile stage) — that's the spray target. Soft scales do 1–2 generations per year; mild winters mean populations carry over. Over-spraying kills the scale predators and populations blow up.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Check stems and leaf undersides for bumps and honeydew (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Prevent
- Keep plants healthy; don't over-fertilize. Stress favors scale buildup.
- Choose resistant plants where known (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Physical
- Hand-wash or scrub heavy infestations.
- Prune out infested branches (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Biological
- Twice-stabbed lady beetles eat scale — spare them.
- Manage ants; they protect scale and chase off the natural enemies (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).
### Least-toxic products

![Horticultural oil](../images/products/placeholder/horticultural-oil.svg) *Horticultural oil — add your own product photo.*

- Horticultural oil (dormant-season) and insecticidal soap. Coverage matters — soak the undersides (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Soil systemics (imidacloprid/dinotefuran) work on soft scale only — NOT armored scale.
- Not on edible crops without label permission (Refs: TX A&M, UC IPM).

## Field notes (personal)

- One dormant-season oil in February brought a nearly dead Meyer lemon back.
- Partial exit holes in scale shells = parasitic wasps winning; leave it alone.
