# Asian Citrus Psyllid (Diaphorina citri)

- **Type:** sucking — and the vector of a citrus-killing disease
- **Houston timing:** year-round
- **Severity:** HIGH — do not ignore

## Why it matters

The psyllid spreads citrus greening (Huanglongbing, HLB). There is **no cure** — infected trees decline and eventually die, and the psyllid keeps spreading it (Ref: UC IPM).

## Identification

![Asian citrus psyllid adult](../images/bugs/asian-citrus-psyllid.jpg)
*Asian citrus psyllid adult, showing the 45° wing posture. [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Asian_Citrus_Psyllid_adult.jpg) — David Hall, public domain.*

- Adult: tiny (~3 mm), mottled brown, holds wings in a 45° "tent" — the near-perfect ID.
- Nymphs: flat, yellow-orange, with waxy fringe on leaf edges of new flush.

### Look-alikes
- Whiteflies: white, fly in a cloud, no wing-tent.
- Scale: motionless bumps, never jump.

## Host plants

All citrus.

## Signs of damage

1. The psyllid itself on new citrus flush.
2. Distorted new growth with waxy filaments.
3. HLB (months later): blotchy yellow mottling, lopsided bitter fruit, premature drop, decline.

## Texas rules (Ref: TX A&M Plant Clinic)

- Citrus greening is under quarantine in Texas citrus counties.
- Report suspected psyllids or greening to the Texas Department of Agriculture.
- Don't move citrus plants or cuttings out of quarantine areas.
- Buy only certified nursery trees.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Weekly check of new flush; look for the 45° posture and waxy filaments.

### Prevent
- Buy certified citrus only.
- Keep trees healthy and uncrowded — heavy flush attracts them.

### Biological
- Tamarixia radiata (parasitic wasp) controls them; avoid broad-spectrum sprays or you kill this helper (Ref: UC IPM).
### Least-toxic products

![Horticultural oil](../images/products/placeholder/horticultural-oil.svg) *Horticultural oil — add your own product photo.*

- Horticultural oil on the flush suppresses eggs and nymphs. Spray new growth; not during bloom (Ref: UC IPM).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Soil-applied imidacloprid once a year, per label, timed to new flush.
- Never spray during bloom — bees (Ref: UC IPM).

### Treat, don't "cure"
- Treatments suppress the psyllid but do NOT cure HLB. A sick tree should be removed and reported (Ref: TX A&M Plant Clinic).
