# Mealybugs (Pseudococcidae)

- **Type:** sucking
- **Houston timing:** year-round; mostly houseplants and greenhouse plants
- **Severity:** low–medium

## Identification

- Small (2–4 mm) insects wrapped in white cottony wax.
- Found at leaf joints, along stems, on leaf undersides.

### Look-alikes
- Scale: flat bumps, no cottony coat.
- Woolly aphids: similar fluff but on elms/maples.

## Host plants

Houseplants, potted herbs, citrus, gardenias, hibiscus.

## Signs of damage

1. White cottony clumps on stems and leaf joints.
2. Sticky honeydew + sooty mold.
3. Yellowing, distorted leaves.

## Lifecycle in Houston

Many species are all-female and don't need mating. One missed female = a new colony. Generations stack every 3–4 weeks; indoors they breed forever.

## Control (IPM)

### Monitor
- Check new plants weekly until you trust them. Isolate new arrivals (Ref: UC IPM).

### Prevent
- Inspect nursery stock before buying.
- Don't overwater or overfeed — stress and soft growth attract them (Refs: TX A&M, LSU).

### Physical
- Dab each bug and egg sac with 70% rubbing alcohol — it dissolves their wax and kills them. Repeat every 5–7 days (Ref: UC IPM).
- Wipe off colonies; prune out hard-hit tips.

### Biological
- Preserve predatory lady beetles and parasitic wasps.
- Avoid broad-spectrum sprays (Refs: TX A&M, LSU).

### Least-toxic products
- Horticultural oil, insecticidal soap, neem (Ref: UC IPM).

### Conventional (last resort)
- Soil-systemic imidacloprid on ornamental non-edible houseplants only, per label.

## Field notes (personal)

- The 2-week quarantine on all new plants stopped my repeat outbreaks.
- Three alcohol passes, 5 days apart, cleared the jade plant.
